# Temporary accommodation market picture, England: phase 0

Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0. Licence: https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/

Built only from published aggregate statistics: households in temporary accommodation (TA) at each quarter end (MHCLG statutory homelessness detailed local authority tables, sheet TA1) and council TA spend (MHCLG revenue outturn, form RO4, lines 80 to 86). No household-level data. Nothing here assesses any council.

Generated by `scripts/build_picture.py` from `data/processed/`; every number below is computed there, and definitions are in `tamarket/picture.py`. Sources are listed at the end.

## What could make these numbers wrong

1. **Councils missing from the household counts.** In 2024-25, 15 councils lack two or more of the five quarter-end counts; 3 London boroughs have none at all. Together they hold £470.7m of the £2.646bn councils reported on accommodation lines 80 to 85, and no cost per household can be worked out for them. The London comparison covers 24 of the 33 London councils (32 boroughs and the City). Across England, 15 councils lack counts, 79 have fewer than 50 households on average and 8 are left out until a person checks their figures (points 2 and 7). [Certain]
2. **Spend that may be booked outside the TA lines.** 3 councils in 2024-25 and 7 in 2025-26 house at least 100 households in TA on average but report nothing on any of lines 80 to 86. They are left out of comparisons, and very low figures are shown as recording checks, not as low cost. [Certain that the lines are empty; Likely that the spend sits elsewhere]
3. **The spend and the count may cover different households.** RO4 spend can include placements that TA1 does not count (TA1 counts households placed under the statutory homelessness duties), so some high spend per household may reflect a wider group of households, not a higher price. [Likely]
4. **2025-26 is a first release.** MHCLG's first release covers 377 of 413 authorities and estimates the missing returns in its England totals; here 25 of the 296 housing authorities have no return, and the England row exceeds the sum of returns by £36.0m on lines 80 to 85 (£42.9m on 80 to 86). MHCLG also reports large changes within the TA sub-categories, which it puts down to reporting changes by three London boroughs. [Certain, from MHCLG's release and technical notes] National 2025-26 spend and the 2025-26 by-type figures will probably move at the second release, which MHCLG schedules for December. [Likely]
5. **By-type figures depend on how councils classify spend.** The 2025-26 national gross spend per household in B&B (£60,150) against £41,180 a year earlier, and in nightly-paid accommodation (£19,200 against £26,920), point to reclassification rather than price change. At council level, 14 compared councils in 2024-25 and 27 in 2025-26 put more than a quarter of their accommodation spend on types where they report fewer than one household of that type on average. Totals per household are unaffected; by-type comparisons between councils are not safe, so use 2024-25 for national by-type figures. [Certain on the counts; Likely on the cause]
6. **The yearly average is an estimate.** Households in TA are counted on four days a year, and the average assumes a straight line between counts. Where one of five counts is missing it is interpolated, and the council is marked as estimated (17 compared councils in 2024-25, 22 in 2025-26). Over every council with one count missing, compared or not (23 in 2024-25, 32 in 2025-26), filling the gap by carrying the previous count forward instead moves the average by a median of 0.7% (largest 12.8%) in 2024-25 and 0.1% (largest 5.9%) in 2025-26. [Certain on the method and these figures; Likely that the effect on group figures is small]
7. **Some counts jump between quarters.** 7 quarter-on-quarter changes of more than 40% occur in councils averaging at least 100 households; 4 of them are one-quarter dips or their recovery. A dip lowers that council's average for the year. These may be reporting slips or real moves; a person needs to check them before any council's figure is used. [Certain on the counts]
8. **England and regional counts are MHCLG estimates.** They are weighted for non-response and rounded to the nearest 10; council counts are as submitted. [Certain]
9. **All money is cash (nominal), not adjusted for inflation.** [Certain]

## Inputs for the savings model (set by the user, never presented as fact)

Observed national averages, 2024-25: net spend per household-year £15,130 in nightly-paid self-contained accommodation and £8,000 in private sector leased accommodation (gross £26,920 and £21,150). The difference between them is not a saving: the households, places and lease costs differ, and leasing has its own set-up and void costs. The model takes the gap per household-year and the number of households as inputs and only does the arithmetic. Illustration only: 100 households times a £1,000 gap is £100,000 a year. [Certain on the arithmetic; the gap is the user's]

## Method and files

- Raw extracts: `data/raw/browser/*.txt`, each checked against `data/raw/browser/MANIFEST.csv` (its own SHA-256, and the source file's name, size and SHA-256), its row count and its column sums. Ingest log: `data/ingest_log.jsonl`.
- Processed: `data/processed/ta1_wide.csv`, `ta_households.csv`, `ro4_wide.csv`, `ta_spend.csv`, `picture_councils_<year>.csv`, `picture_summary.json`.
- Rebuild: `python scripts/build_from_extracts.py && python scripts/build_picture.py`.
- Two metropolitan councils changed codes on 1 April 2025; both years use the new codes (`tamarket/picture.py`, RECODES).
- RO4 classes kept: shire districts, unitaries, metropolitan districts, London boroughs. County councils and other authorities with small TA lines are in the England row but not in council tables.
- Rounding: money per household to the nearest £10, £m to one decimal place, £bn to three, halves rounded up.
