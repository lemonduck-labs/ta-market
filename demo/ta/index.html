    How the figures are made

    • Households: MHCLG's quarterly count of households in temporary accommodation (table TA1), averaged over the financial year from five quarter-end counts, 31 March to 31 March.
    • Spend: council revenue outturn, form RO4, lines 80 to 85 for accommodation and line 86 for administration. Gross is total spend; net is after income such as housing benefit.
    • Compared councils: at least four of the five counts, positive spend on lines 80 to 85 and at least 50 households on average. Peer groups: London (its 33 councils) and the rest of England.
    • Councils shown: the compared councils, less those with a data check outstanding: a change of more than 40% from one quarter-end count to the next (counts held from March 2024 to March 2026; councils averaging at least 100 households), or nothing on lines 80 to 86 in 2024-25 or 2025-26 despite at least 100 households. They are counted, not shown, until a person checks them.
    • The median, band and share of peers lower beside an example are over the other councils shown in its peer group, the grey dots, not the example itself. The example is chosen as the council closest to the median of all the councils shown.
    • Examples: in each peer group, among the councils shown with all five counts and at least 500 households (London) or 200 (rest of England), the council closest to the median; a tie goes to the lower gross figure. The examples and the dots are unnamed, not anonymous: each shows a council's own published figures, so anyone with MHCLG's tables can match it to its council [Certain].
    • Rounding: example money to the nearest £100; peers' and England's money per household to £10; example households to 10 below 1,000 and to 50 above; peers' households to 10; shares to whole per cent, adjusted so each column adds to 100% (a line with a credit shows a negative share); halves rounded up.

    What could make these numbers wrong

    • Some councils are missing counts or report nothing on the TA lines, so they cannot be compared [Certain].
    • RO4 spend can cover placements the TA1 count does not, so a high figure can reflect a wider group of households, not a higher price [Likely].
    • Councils classify spend differently between types, so compare totals, not types [Likely]. Council or housing association stock is mostly costed outside these lines [Likely].
    • Councils averaging at least 100 households whose count changes by more than 40% between quarters are left out of the dots until a person checks them; smaller councils are not left out for such a change. A change that size may be a reporting slip or a real move [Likely; Certain on the counts].
    • 2025-26 spend is a first release covering 377 of 413 authorities, with the rest estimated by MHCLG; the second release is due in December 2026 [Certain, as MHCLG states it].
    • The 31 March 2026 count is expected to be revised with the April to June 2026 release, announced provisionally for 29 October 2026 [Likely for the revision; Certain for the date as announced].
    • The yearly average assumes a straight line between counts. A missing count is estimated between its neighbours or, for the first or last count, copied from the nearest one [Certain on the method].
    • England and regional counts are MHCLG estimates, weighted for non-response and rounded to the nearest 10; council counts are as submitted [Certain].
    • All money is cash, not adjusted for inflation [Certain].

    What it does

    Move forward has two parts. This market picture shows what a council in England spends on temporary accommodation and on what kind, set against the councils shown in its group (London’s 33 councils, or the rest of England), none named. The planner helps housing officers plan each household’s route out into a settled home, with a second officer approving each plan.

    Who uses it

    • A team manager or commissioner reads this picture: where the council’s spend per household and its mix sit among its peers, and what questions that raises.
    • A housing officer uses the planner for each household’s plan.
    • A second officer approves each plan there, returns it or rejects it.

    How to read the picture

    1. Your council: pick the example council, then read its four figures, the spread of its peers (one dot each, unnamed), its accommodation mix and the questions they raise.
    2. The savings illustration: set the price gap between nightly-paid and leased homes yourself. It is an input, never a fact.
    3. England and Method and caveats: the national trend, and what each figure is and is not.

    What is demo data

    The figures here are published aggregates: MHCLG’s homelessness statistics (table TA1) and revenue outturn (RO4). No council is named, but none is anonymous either, as each shows its own published figures. Every household in the planner is synthetic.

    What a deployment could add

    • New figures each quarter, the picture rebuilt when MHCLG publishes.
    • The council’s own prices in the savings illustration, and its own peer group if it wants one.
    • For the planner: the council’s own caseload, sign-in and roles. A hosted version is built and tested, not deployed.

    Improving it

    This is a first build on published data, and it can be improved further: each quarter’s release as it comes, spend broken down where councils publish more detail, and the planner’s checks widened beyond the selection it holds now.
